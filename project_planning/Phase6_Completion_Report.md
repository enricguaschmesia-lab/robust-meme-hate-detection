# Phase 6 — Failure-case narrative

> Status: written 2026-05-18 on the 500-example dev split, drawing on
> the per-example data from all 15 (recipe × seed) pairs of
> Phase 5/5b/5c. The supporting tables, bucket counts, and example IDs
> live in `project_planning/phase4/failure_analysis.md` (regenerated by
> `scripts/failure_analysis.py`, the multi-seed / multi-recipe /
> composite-aware variant). The dev metrics this report grounds itself
> in are in `project_planning/phase4/robust_vs_clean.md`.

## 1. What this report does

Phase 5's headline tables answer "*how much* does each robust recipe
move the average per-cell ΔAUROC and per-example survival counts."
Phase 6 answers two complementary questions the averages hide:

1. **Where does the *residual* fragility live?** Which dev examples
   does every recipe in the sweep still fail naturally — and what do
   they have in common?
2. **Where do the Pareto winners (`kl`, `kldrop`) actually disagree
   per-example, and what's the qualitative pattern of the disagreement?**

Both questions are answered against a per-example, per-recipe-seed
table built from the perturbed-eval and whitebox-eval JSONs. The table
buckets each (example, recipe, seed) triple into one of five categories:

- `B1_robust` — clean-correct AND survives every single-cell natural
  AND every composite natural AND PGD ε=4/255. The strictest possible
  "fully bulletproof" bucket.
- `B2_clean_wrong` — wrong before any attack.
- `B3_natural_attack_flipped` — clean-correct, flipped by ≥ 1
  single-cell natural attack.
- `B5_composite_only_failure` — clean-correct, robust to single-cell
  naturals, flipped only by a composite attack.
- `B4_pgd_only` — clean-correct, robust to *all* naturals (single +
  composite), broken only by PGD.

Buckets are computed per (recipe, seed), then the recipe's per-example
"majority bucket" across the 3 seeds is used for the cross-recipe
comparisons. This is the right unit for the project's threat model:
the *expected* status of an example under that recipe.

The naturalistic-fully-robust column reported in
`robust_vs_clean.md` corresponds to `B1 + B4` (an example is in B4 iff
it survives every natural attack and only PGD breaks it).
`B5` quantifies the *composite-specific* attack surface that the
single-cell-only definition (`B1 + B4 + B5`) misses.

## 2. Bucket counts across recipes

3-seed mean ± σ, multimodal at per-run best macro-F1 threshold.

| Bucket | `clean` | `augonly` | `kl` | `kldrop` | `kllowmed` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B1 (everything) | 0.0 | 1.7 | 1.0 | 0.3 | 1.3 |
| **B2 (clean-wrong)** | 152.0 | 165.3 | 156.0 | **171.3** | 153.0 |
| **B3 (single-cell broken)** | **243.3** | 220.0 | 209.7 | **204.7** | 214.3 |
| B5 (composite-only broken) | 14.3 | 27.3 | 22.7 | **32.3** | 25.3 |
| B4 (PGD-only broken) | 90.3 | 85.7 | **110.7** | 91.3 | 106.0 |

Three things in this table are non-obvious and worth flagging:

- **B5 count goes *up* with robustness, not down.** `kldrop` has the
  most composite-only failures (32 vs 14 for clean). That is **not**
  a regression — it's a saturation artefact. As single-cell robustness
  improves, more examples *survive* single-cell attacks and reach the
  composite gate at all. The headline composite metric (ΔAUROC and
  ASR in `phase4/robust_vs_clean.md`) still has `kldrop` as the Pareto
  winner on every composite cell. B5 is a per-example *count*, not a
  per-cell ΔAUROC.
- **`kldrop` raises B2 (clean-wrong) the most** (+19 vs clean) — the
  clean-accuracy cost the headline numbers also report (0.742 → 0.705
  AUROC) shows up here as 19 additional examples the recipe just gets
  wrong before any attack. This is the irreducible cost of forcing the
  fusion head to commit to image-only predictions 30 % of the time.
- **`kl` has the largest naturalistic-robust count** (B1 + B4 = 111.7
  vs 90.3 clean, +28 %) without paying meaningful clean-accuracy cost.
  Consistent with `kl` being the clean-accuracy-preserving Pareto
  winner under the single-attack threat model.

## 3. Consensus residual hotspots — what every recipe still fails on

**331 / 500 dev examples** are naturally failing (majority B2 or B3
across seeds) under **every one** of the five recipes. That is the
size of the residual failure set no recipe in our sweep solves.

Reading the 15 representative entries in
`phase4/failure_analysis.md` §2, three failure modes dominate:

1. **Caption-erasing text attacks on already-borderline non-hate
   examples** (label=0, examples like `id=02145`, `id=03798`,
   `id=04538`, `id=06352`). Clean prob is in the [0.3, 0.6] range —
   close to the threshold — and `censoring@high` /
   `char_deletion@high` / `keyboard_typo@high` push them across. The
   image-text fusion head is too dependent on the caption tokens, so
   when the tokens are erased it tips the wrong way. Even `kldrop`
   (which has the strongest image branch) doesn't save them, because
   the *clean* margin is already small; what `kldrop` saves are
   non-hate cases with a larger image-anchored margin.
2. **Clean-wrong examples on subtle, context-dependent text content**
   (`id=03214`, `id=04621`, `id=05213`, `id=07351`). These are
   captions where the hateful interpretation depends on world
   knowledge or fine-grained cultural reading the OpenCLIP text
   encoder doesn't expose; the model misreads them at clean and every
   recipe inherits that miss. Robust training can't fix what's
   missing from the encoder. (Some entries here also look like
   genuine *label noise* — `id=03214`'s caption is plausibly read as
   either dark humour or a borderline-hate inference depending on the
   annotator's threshold.)
3. **Single-token slurs hidden by character-level perturbation on
   hate examples** (label=1). `leetspeak@high` and `char_swap@high`
   on captions whose hate signal is concentrated in 1–2 tokens.
   Augmentation does dampen these (B3 count drops 243 → 205 from
   clean → kldrop) but doesn't eliminate the failure mode.

The reading: **residual failures are predominantly text-fragile and
encoder-bottlenecked**, not modelling-defect-bottlenecked. A robust
recipe that wanted to push the consensus failure count below ~200
would likely need to either (a) augment with a stronger text encoder
or (b) add a representation-level robustness loss that operates on
embedded text rather than on raw character edits. The current
augmentation + KL framework saturates on the symbolic-edit surface.

## 4. Where `kldrop` and `kl` actually disagree per-example

The headline tables show `kldrop` is the strict Pareto winner under
composite attacks while `kl` is the clean-accuracy-preserving winner
under single-attack threats. Per-example data refines this to a
**class-asymmetric** story.

**`kldrop` beats `kl` on 34 examples**, *33 of which are label=0*
(non-hate) and 1 label=1. Pattern: examples where `kl`'s clean prob is low (e.g.
0.05–0.21) but its threshold is calibrated at τ=0.40, so the margin
is small; a text attack — usually `leetspeak@high` or
`char_deletion@medium` — bumps the attacked prob into the [0.4, 0.6]
range and flips the prediction to "hate." Under `kldrop` the
*image branch carries an anchor* against the false-positive bump:
clean probs are similarly small, but the image embedding pulls the
post-attack prob back down. Representative examples:

- `id=01742` (`"in just one hour from now i'll only have 4 hours
  left..."`): `kl` clean 0.050, `leetspeak@high` → 0.447 (flip).
  `kldrop` clean 0.079, attacked stays sub-threshold.
- `id=09863` (`"diverse group of women"`): `kl` clean 0.064,
  `char_deletion@medium` → 0.415 (flip). `kldrop` clean 0.056,
  attacked sub-threshold.
- `id=09152` (`"i don't have ex's. i have y's..."`):
  `kl` clean 0.201, `char_deletion@medium` → 0.415. `kldrop` clean
  0.230, attacked sub-threshold.

This is the *image-branch revival* finding made concrete: 34 dev
non-hate examples are saved by `kldrop` specifically because its
fusion head can fall back on a usable image-only signal when the
text is being attacked.

**`kl` beats `kldrop` on 49 examples**, *46 of which are label=1*
(hate) and 3 label=0.
Pattern: examples where `kldrop`'s lower clean accuracy puts the
clean prob just *barely* on the hate side, and a text attack erodes
the margin enough to flip. `kl`'s higher clean confidence absorbs
the same attack. Representative:

- `id=04857` (`"...even the dumb jews know that! trump is a racist."`):
  `kldrop` clean 0.861 — B5 (broken only by composite); `kl` clean
  0.812 — B4 (composite-survival, only PGD breaks it). Both are
  clean-correct but `kldrop` falls to a composite the `kl` head
  resists.
- `id=05126` (`"can you smell what the reich is cooking"`):
  `kldrop` clean 0.548, `char_deletion@high` → 0.412 (flip). `kl`
  clean 0.588, attacked stays above 0.40.
- `id=07198` (`"islam is a religion of peace stop criticizing my
  religion"`): `kldrop` clean 0.508, `censoring@high` → 0.523. The
  small clean-side margin (0.508 − 0.48 = 0.028) is the entire
  story; `kl`'s 0.695 clean prob absorbs the same attack.

**Joint reading.** `kldrop` and `kl` are not just "two points on a
clean-accuracy/robustness Pareto front" — they are recipes that
trade off in *different directions on different classes*.
`kldrop`'s image-branch revival pays back on label=0 cases where
text attacks would otherwise be false-positive-inducing; `kl`'s
clean-accuracy preservation pays back on label=1 cases where the
text signal is strong but borderline-confident under `kldrop`.
A deployment that cares about **false positives on harmless memes**
(consumer-facing moderation) should prefer `kldrop`; one that cares
about **catching genuinely hateful memes that don't shout** (a
safety-floor classifier behind human review) should prefer `kl`.
This is a defensible per-class deployment recommendation and the
strongest qualitative finding of Phase 6.

## 5. Composite-only failures (B5)

| Recipe | B5 count |
|---|---:|
| `clean` | 9 |
| `augonly` | 27 |
| `kl` | 17 |
| `kldrop` | 32 |
| `kllowmed` | 23 |

As called out in §2, the B5 *count* is monotone-but-misleading.
What B5 actually surfaces is the *composition* of the residual
attack surface for each recipe:

- **For `clean`**, B5 is tiny (9) because most clean-correct examples
  are already broken by single-cell attacks before composites get a
  chance — only 9 examples are robust enough to "make it" to the
  composite gate and then fail.
- **For `kldrop`**, B5 swells to 32 because 32 examples now survive
  every single-cell attack but get caught by a composite. Looking at
  the per-example composite probs in `failure_analysis.md` §5, the
  dominant composite breaking them is `composite_2text` followed by
  `composite_2text_2image` — two text edits on the same caption
  exceed what one text edit can do. Image-side composites
  (`composite_2image`) break these examples least often, consistent
  with `kldrop`'s revived image branch carrying signal that survives
  image-only edits.

Representative `kldrop` B5 example: `id=03745`
(`"your order comes to $37.50 and your white privilege discount
brings the total to $37.50"`, label=1) — survives every single-cell
cell, but `composite_2text` mean attacked prob = 0.412 (below τ),
and `composite_2text_2image` = 0.306 (well below). The composite is
doing real adversarial work: combining two character-level edits
strips enough of the hate-signalling tokens that the image alone
can't carry the prediction. This is the threat model composite cells
were designed to surface.

Per recipe, the realistic-threat takeaway from B5:

- Single-cell augmentation is *necessary but not sufficient*. The
  composite gate filters out a real chunk of "looks robust" examples.
- Doubling up text attacks (`composite_2text`) is the most damaging
  composite type — consistent with the overall `composite_2text`
  ΔAUROC numbers in `robust_vs_clean.md` (e.g. `kldrop` medium 0.051,
  highest of all composite types).
- The 32 B5 examples under `kldrop` are an explicit list of where a
  future training intervention could focus: most are caption-erasing
  + character-level combinations on examples with weak image-only
  signal.

## 6. What this analysis *cannot* say

- **Single dev split (n=500), 3 seeds per recipe.** Disagreement
  counts (34 / 49 / 32) are point estimates with no confidence
  interval; bucket transitions on the borderline have non-trivial
  seed noise. The class-asymmetric finding (kldrop-wins are label=0,
  kl-wins are label=1) is *qualitatively* clean — every kldrop-win
  example I sampled is label=0 — but the size of that effect would
  need test-set replication (Phase 7) to claim with a number.
- **"B2 (clean-wrong)" is a mixed bag.** Some examples are genuine
  model failures (the model misreads them); some are *label noise*
  in the dataset itself. The consensus failure set has at least a
  handful of items where reasonable annotators could disagree
  (`id=03214`, `id=05213`). The 331 consensus count is therefore an
  *upper bound* on the modelling-defect residual.
- **The bucket transitions don't account for borderline-stable
  predictions.** A prediction with prob = 0.48 (right at τ=0.48)
  classed as B3 is barely a failure; the bucket boundary is hard.
  A continuous "margin loss" metric per recipe would be a useful
  follow-up.
- **No image-side qualitative analysis.** The narrative leans heavily
  on text-attack stories because text cells dominate the residual
  failure surface (per-family ΔAUROC text ≈ 5–10× the image-pixel/
  photometric/geometric numbers). The image-side failures exist but
  are too quiet to surface in the consensus-failure listing.
- **`kllowmed` is reported in the bucket-counts table but not in the
  narrative.** It's intentionally treated as a methodology check —
  see Phase 5c §13.5 for the negative-result writeup.

## 7. What to take into Phase 7

The Phase 6 analysis suggests three explicit hypotheses to look for
on the held-out test split:

1. **Class-asymmetric kldrop/kl trade-off should reproduce.** kldrop's
   B1/B4 majority should be label=0-skewed vs kl on test; kl's
   should be label=1-skewed. If this reproduces, it is the
   strongest single-recipe deployment-conditional finding in the
   project.
2. **Composite-2text is the dominant residual threat for every
   recipe**, so the test-side composite_2text ΔAUROC should track
   the dev numbers within seed noise. If it diverges, the composite
   cell design itself (per-sample component sampling) might be
   over-tuned to the dev pool.
3. **The 331 consensus naturally-failing dev examples should have a
   counterpart count on test** of roughly comparable proportion
   (~50–66 % of test ex). If much higher, robust gains are dev-overfit.
