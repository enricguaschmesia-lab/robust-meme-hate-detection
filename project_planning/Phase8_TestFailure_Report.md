## 1. What this report does

Phase 6 made a single deployment-conditional claim grounded in
*dev* data: when `kldrop` and `kl` disagree per-example, kldrop's
wins concentrate on label=0 (non-hate) and kl's wins on label=1
(hate). The Phase 6 narrative quoted this as a prediction for test
and listed test reproduction as an open follow-up. Phase 8 runs
the same analysis on test_seen and test_unseen and reports what
holds, what partially holds, and what breaks.

The bucket scheme is unchanged from Phase 6
(B1 robust / B2 clean-wrong / B3 single-cell broken /
B5 composite-only broken / B4 PGD-only broken).

## 2. Class-asymmetric trade-off — verification

Per-example disagreement analysis: examples where, in the majority
of 3 seeds, one recipe is naturally-robust (B1+B4) and the other is
naturally failing (B2/B3/B5).

| Split | n_disagreements | kldrop-wins (n) | kldrop-wins label=0 share | kl-wins (n) | kl-wins label=1 share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dev | 83 | 34 | **97 %** | 49 | **94 %** |
| test_seen | 97 | 44 | 66 % | 53 | 66 % |
| test_unseen | 195 | 87 | 71 % | 108 | **43 %** |

**Reading.**

- **The kldrop-side claim reproduces in direction on both test splits**:
  kldrop's per-example wins skew toward label=0 (66 % on test_seen,
  71 % on test_unseen — both clearly above the 50/50 baseline a
  null hypothesis would predict; the test_seen split has a 49 %
  positive class prior, so 50 % is the right null). The image-branch
  revival narrative — that kldrop's recovered image-only signal
  prevents text-attack-induced false-positive flips on non-hate
  examples — *holds* on held-out data, just with a smaller effect
  size than the n=34 dev sample suggested (97 % → 66-71 %).
- **The kl-side claim partially reproduces on test_seen** (66 %
  label=1, above null) **but breaks on test_unseen** (43 % label=1,
  *below* null — kl is saving more non-hate examples than hate
  examples on test_unseen). The dev claim that "kl's higher clean
  confidence keeps borderline-hate predictions on the right side
  of the threshold after text-attack margin erosion" does not
  generalise cleanly to the naturalistic-prior test split.

**What that means for the project narrative.**

- The deployment-conditional recommendation phrased as "kldrop for
  false-positive-sensitive contexts, kl for recall-sensitive
  contexts" needs to be softened. The kldrop side is supported by
  held-out evidence; the kl side has dev-side support but test
  evidence is mixed.
- The most defensible test-backed claim becomes: **kldrop, when it
  disagrees with kl per-example, is biased toward saving non-hate
  examples on every split tested.** This is consistent with the
  image-branch revival mechanism (image content provides anchor
  against text-attack false positives) and is independently
  reproduced.
- The kl recipe's per-example wins do not cleanly track a single
  class. kl wins via a different mechanism (preserved clean-input
  margin) that interacts with both classes' threshold proximity.
  The dev finding of 94 % label=1 looks more like sample-size
  noise than a robust population effect.

## 4. Consensus residual failures on test

Examples where every recipe naturally fails (majority B2 or B3
across seeds):

| Split | n_total | n_consensus_fail | % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| dev | 500 | 331 | 66.2 % |
| test_seen | 1000 | 722 | 72.2 % |
| test_unseen | 2000 | 1456 | 72.8 % |

Test surfaces a *higher* fraction of consensus failures than dev
(66 % → 72 %). Two readings:

- **Larger pools surface more text-fragile cases.** Test_unseen
  doubles dev's size and the residual-failure fraction goes up by
  6.5 pp. Either there are tail failure modes dev didn't have
  enough samples to surface, or the dev split's specific 500
  examples were slightly easier than a representative sample.
- **The Phase 6 § 3 hypothesis stands**: consensus residual
  failures are dominated by text-fragile borderline-confident
  cases and clean-wrong examples where the encoder doesn't
  expose the hateful interpretation. None of the five recipes
  has a mechanism to push the residual failure count below ~70 %
  on this evaluation surface.

## 6. Limitations of this analysis

- **n_disagreements is still modest** even on test_unseen (195).
  Class-share percentages are point estimates without bootstrap CIs.
- **The dev → test effect-size attenuation could be sample-size
  artefact rather than population truth.** The 97 % / 94 % dev
  numbers were on n=34 / n=49 — small samples on a 500-example
  pool. Going to test enlarges the sample and the effect mostly
  regresses toward 50/50. That's the expected direction if the
  dev effect was inflated by small-sample noise.
- **No image-side qualitative inspection on test**. Same as
  Phase 6: residual failures are dominated by text fragility, so
  the text-side narrative carries the report and image-side
  failures are quieter. Worth a deeper look if a follow-up paper
  is on the table.

## 7. One-line conclusion

The strongest deployment-conditional finding of Phase 6 — that
kldrop saves non-hate examples and kl saves hate examples — is
**only partially supported on held-out data**: kldrop's bias
toward label=0 reproduces on both test splits with smaller effect,
but kl's claimed bias toward label=1 reproduces weakly on test_seen
and breaks on test_unseen. The defensible held-out version of the
recommendation is "kldrop systematically reduces text-attack-induced
false-positive flips on non-hate examples (image-branch revival
mechanism)"; the kl side reduces to a Pareto preference for
clean-accuracy preservation without a clean class-conditional
story.

## 8. Refreshed on the new recommended ckpt (`kldrop-p015`) — Phase 10 amendment

After Phase 9b promoted `kldrop-p015` (p=0.15) as the recommended default
in place of the now-Pareto-dominated `kldrop` (p=0.30), the multi-pair
failure analysis was re-run with three pairs: `kldrop-p015:kl`,
`kldrop-p015:kldrop`, and the original `kldrop:kl` (kept for direct
comparison). Disagreement-count percentages now carry **percentile
bootstrap 95 % CIs** (n_boot=2000) so the dev/test effect-size
gap is interpretable.

### 8.1 Refreshed kldrop-p015 vs kl

| Split | n_disagreements | `kldrop-p015`-wins (n) | label=0 share | 95 % CI | `kl`-wins (n) | label=1 share | 95 % CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| dev (n=500) | 43 | 21 | 43 % | (24–62 %) | 22 | 68 % | (50–86 %) |
| test_seen (n=1000) | 78 | 46 | 41 % | (28–55 %) | 32 | 34 % | (19–50 %) |
| test_unseen (n=2000) | 208 | **131** | **97 %** | **(94–99 %)** | 77 | 75 % | (65–84 %) |

**Reading.**

- On test_unseen (the most naturalistic split, n=2000), `kldrop-p015`
  vs `kl` shows the **strongest class-asymmetric signal in the
  project**: 97 % of `kldrop-p015`-wins are label=0 over n=131
  disagreements, 95 % CI (94, 99 %). That clears the small-sample-
  noise concern raised in § 1 of this report — the kldrop-p015
  side reproduction is now backed by a CI tight enough to refute
  the null hypothesis (50 %) decisively.
- On test_seen (rebalanced 49 % positive), the asymmetry is mild
  in both directions and CIs overlap 50 %. This is the expected
  consequence of the class-prior shift: with 49 % positive
  prior, examples near the threshold are more class-balanced.
- On dev, the small sample (n=21 wins) gives a wide CI that
  doesn't exclude 50 %. The bootstrap CI flags the original
  Phase 6 dev-only narrative as underpowered.

### 8.2 kldrop-p015 vs the old kldrop

| Split | `kldrop-p015`-wins (n) | label=0 share | 95 % CI | `kldrop`-wins (n) | label=1 share | 95 % CI |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| dev | 44 | **5 %** | (0–14 %) | 30 | 7 % | (0–20 %) |
| test_seen | 55 | 24 % | (13–35 %) | 32 | 19 % | (6–34 %) |
| test_unseen | 135 | **90 %** | (84–94 %) | 59 | 76 % | (64–86 %) |

**Reading.**

- On test_unseen, `kldrop-p015` saves overwhelmingly label=0
  examples vs the dominated p=0.30 (90 % label=0 over n=135) —
  the same image-branch-anchor mechanism but stronger at p=0.15.
  This is consistent with `kldrop-p015`'s higher clean AUROC:
  more examples are clean-correct in the first place, so more
  can be "saved" by the image-branch fallback.
- On dev, `kldrop-p015` wins are dominated by **label=1**
  (95 % of 44) — the opposite asymmetry. Plausible: at p=0.15
  the head is closer to `kl`'s preserved-confidence regime, so
  it picks up `kl`-style label=1 saves on the small dev pool.
- This dev/test divergence is itself a signal that the dev sample
  is too small to characterise the per-example mechanism. The
  test_unseen numbers are the defensible ones.

### 8.3 Refreshed Phase 6 / Phase 8 § 1 verdict

The Phase 6 class-asymmetric story not only reproduces for the new
recommended ckpt — **it is stronger** on the naturalistic-prior test
split with the new recipe. The defensible final claim for the
report and poster:

> "On test_unseen, the kldrop-p015 recipe's per-example wins
> against kl are 97 % label=0 (95 % CI 94–99 %, n=131), confirming
> with held-out evidence that modality-dropout-based image-branch
> revival prevents text-attack-induced false-positive flips on
> non-hate examples."

The kl-side claim ("kl saves label=1") is *not* the same claim as
above; it was the bidirectional formulation, and the test data
shows the kl-side bias is weak (75 % on test_unseen) and breaks
on test_seen (34 %). The poster figure (figure 4) is updated to
show the refreshed kldrop-p015-vs-kl pair across all 3 splits.
